## 1. Purpose and scope boundary

This protocol joins three remaining questions without allowing one to lend
evidence to another:

1. `PRW-JO1` — can a fixed family of orbit-constrained planks improve the
   complete leading distance/error spectrum?
2. `PRW-A1` — can the next orbit-constrained plank be selected efficiently from
   the current posterior?
3. `PRW-G2` — can the same local orbit states participate in an irreducible
   factor or hypergraph that does not collapse to independent nodes or one
   global phase?

The earlier `PRW-G1` result remains closed in its declared domain. A connected
graph with hard, cycle-consistent constraints

\[
h_v-h_u=g_{uv}\pmod p
\]

has the solution

\[
h_v=z+o_v\pmod p.
\]

Its graph decoder and the explicit global-phase-plus-fixed-offset comparator
were exactly equal in the bounded tests. `PRW-G2` is not a retry of that
mechanism. It admits only factors excluded from the `PRW-G1` model and begins
with an algebraic collapse test.

Nothing in this protocol asserts physical dimensionality, gravity, resonance,
quantum behavior, cheaper training, improved RAG, novelty, or production
utility.

## 2. Shared mathematical object

Let \(G=(V,\mathcal F)\) be a factor or hypergraph. Each node \(v\) has a
declared finite local state

\[
h_v\in\mathcal H_v,
\]

where the first bounded slice uses a cyclic phase in \(\mathbb Z_p\), with any
type, polarity, or payload component declared separately. Let
\(\ell_v(h_v;y_v,q)\) be local evidence produced under one common observation
channel. A factor \(A\in\mathcal F\) acts on the tuple \(h_A\) through
\(\psi_A(h_A;q)\). The frozen score is

\[
S(h\mid y,q)=
\sum_{v\in V}\ell_v(h_v;y_v,q)
+
\sum_{A\in\mathcal F}\psi_A(h_A;q),
\qquad
\widehat h=\arg\max_h S(h\mid y,q).
\]

The graph contributes declared relational information or a factorization of
information already available to the flat control. It does not create evidence
for free.

For a fixed set of \(K\) planks, let \(f_k(h)\) be the binary observation word
for hypothesis \(h\). The concatenated representation is

\[
C(h)=f_1(h)\Vert\cdots\Vert f_K(h),
\]

and its Hamming distance is exactly

\[
d_C(i,j)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_H(f_k(i),f_k(j)).
\]

Therefore a fixed plank stack is one longer code. A flattened signature with
identical observations, rate, bits, storage, and decoder opportunity is a
mandatory control.

## 4. `PRW-JO1` — orbit-constrained joint-plank spectrum

### 4.1 Hypothesis

For every competitor \(i\) to a transmitted state, let \(d_k(i)\) be its
distance on plank \(k\) and

\[
D_K(i)=\sum_k d_k(i).
\]

Let \(\beta_q(d)\) be the frozen BSC tie-or-better tail. Define the
posterior-independent leading union surrogate

\[
\Lambda_q(F)=\sum_{i\in W}\beta_q(D_K(i)),
\]

where \(W\) and all tie semantics are frozen before comparison. Exact finite
event-union probability is preferred whenever admitted by the resource
envelope.

The null is:

\[
H_{0,\mathrm{JO1}}:
\]

the best admissible orbit family is matched or beaten by a flattened signature
or established sequence/interleaver control on the frozen lexicographic
objective:

1. maximize minimum aggregate distance;
2. minimize its multiplicity;
3. minimize the next leading shell mass;
4. minimize exact or bounded event-union probability;
5. minimize decoder work and storage.

The alternative is:

\[
H_{1,\mathrm{JO1}}:
\]

an orbit-constrained family strictly improves that frozen ordering at matched
rate, observations, storage, and decoder opportunity.

Negative Pearson correlation among global shell vectors is not a pass metric.
It can be driven by remote competitors that carry negligible posterior or
error mass.

### 4.2 Required controls

- one flattened signature with the same total coordinates;
- aligned repetition;
- seeded random relabeling;
- a complementary or supplementary-difference construction when admissible;
- a low-correlation frequency-hopping/difference-family control;
- a code-matched interleaver or parallel-concatenated control;
- the best ordinary unconstrained code found within the same search budget;
- identity and duplicated-plank degeneracy controls.

For the current two-type alignment-only abstraction, define

\[
N_{tu}(s)=
\#\{(k,x):\phi_{t,k}(x)-\phi_{u,k}(x)=s\}.
\]

Minimizing only \(\max_sN_{tu}(s)\) is a group-difference packing problem. At
\(p=4691\), eight coordinates per plank can all receive distinct differences
for \(K\le586\). Any surviving claim must therefore involve the complete
typed/masked leading spectrum, decoder semantics, or a systems constraint—not
alignment count alone.

### 4.3 Kill conditions

Kill `PRW-JO1` if:

- a known complementary/difference/interleaver construction matches the result;
- the apparent improvement vanishes under the common channel;

- the construction has no efficient encoder/decoder or proof advantage.

## 5. `PRW-A1` — posterior-guided orbit/plank acquisition

At step \(t\), the posterior is

\[
\pi_t(h)\propto
\pi_{t-1}(h)W_{a_t}(y_t\mid h).
\]

The exact action oracle selects the admissible next plank \(a\) by frozen
expected information gain, EJS per cost, or the Chernoff objective

\[
\max_a
\min_{j\ne\widehat h_t}
\frac{
D\!\left(W_a(\cdot\mid\widehat h_t)
\Vert W_a(\cdot\mid j)\right)}
{\kappa(a)}.
\]

For BSC plank codewords, pairwise KL is a fixed channel constant times Hamming
distance. Posterior-guided acquisition is therefore established controlled
sensing, not a novelty candidate.

`PRW-A1` asks only whether orbit structure supplies:

1. a nondegenerate restricted action family;
2. a fast all-action evaluator; and
3. a matched expected-cost or policy-evaluation advantage.

### Nondegeneracy gate

Before adaptive execution, at least one admissible action pair must change a
posterior-relevant likelihood ratio. If every action is a common
channel-invariant isometry or label permutation, `PRW-A1` is
`ACTION_FAMILY_INFORMATIONALLY_DEGENERATE` and stops.

### Required controls

- exact MaxEJS/Chernoff on the identical action set;
- mutual-information-greedy selection;
- best frozen static schedule selected without REPORT;
- seeded random schedule;
- ordinary incremental redundancy;
- stop-only feedback with the same confidence threshold.

### Pass and kill boundary

A possible systems pass requires either:

- strictly lower expected acquired bits/observations at the same error,
  false-unlock, and compute ceiling; or
- no more than a preregistered tolerance from exact MaxEJS with materially lower
  action-evaluation work.

It is killed if generic active testing matches it, selector work consumes the
acquisition saving, or the action library is degenerate. A pass would still be
an orbit-restricted implementation/result, not a new adaptive-testing
principle.

## 6. `PRW-G2` — irreducible orbit-coded factor/hypergraph mesh

### 6.1 `PRW-G2P` — nonseparable pair factors

For a pair factor, write

\[
J_{uv}(a,b)=\psi_{uv}(a,b).
\]

It is locally nonseparable only if a mixed difference is nonzero for some
\(a,a',b,b'\):

\[
J(a,b)-J(a,b')-J(a',b)+J(a',b')\ne0.
\]

Equivalently, its matrix after removing row, column, and grand means is nonzero.
This rejects unary terms presented as bonds.

Local nonseparability is necessary but not sufficient. The complete factor
family must also fail reduction to:

- independent node scores;
- one global phase plus fixed offsets;
- a flat sufficient statistic with equal or lower cost; or
- a standard factorization whose established decoder matches the claimed
  advantage.

### 6.2 `PRW-G2H` — higher-order hyperfactors

For \(|A|\ge3\), the highest-order functional-ANOVA/Möbius interaction of
\(\psi_A\) must be nonzero after all strict-subset terms are removed. A modular
constraint such as

\[
a h_u+b h_v+c h_w=r\pmod p
\]

is admissible only if a pairwise auxiliary-variable reduction does not match or
beat it after auxiliary states, factor-table bytes, and operations are charged.

### 6.3 `PRW-G2Q` — query-conditioned factors

A query-conditioned mesh must change the interaction itself. Query-dependent
unary biases are not changing bonds. For some \(q,q'\), the highest-order
interaction residual of

\[
\psi_A(\cdot;q)-\psi_A(\cdot;q')
\]

must be nonzero. The query representation, branching information, factor
construction, and training cost are charged.

### 6.4 Static hypothesis

The null is:

\[
H_{0,\mathrm{G2}}:
\]

the factor family is separable, gauge/global-phase reducible, strict-subset
reducible, or matched by flat MAP and established factor-graph decoding.

The alternative is:

\[
H_{1,\mathrm{G2}}:
\]

the irreducible orbit family improves the frozen accuracy/error versus
storage/decoder-work frontier over all information-matched controls.

The primary synthetic question is not whether exact flat MAP and exact
factorized MAP disagree; when they receive the same score function they must
agree. The question is whether the factorization or orbit structure enables a
provable construction, compact representation, or approximate decoder that
reaches the exact result with less matched work.

### 6.5 Required controls

- independent unary nodes;
- zero-factor graph;
- the closed `PRW-G1` global-phase-plus-offset family;
- exact flat MAP receiving the same factors and observations;
- degree/topology-matched random factors;
- shuffled factor labels;
- equal-complexity wrong grouping;
- pairwise auxiliary reduction for every hyperfactor;
- exact junction-tree/sum-product when tractable;
- loopy and residual belief propagation;
- beam search at the same operation budget;
- conventional noncyclic metadata/product-key factors.

### 6.6 Kill conditions

Kill the relevant `PRW-G2` sublane if:

- interaction residuals are zero;
- the complete model collapses to `PRW-G1` or independent nodes;
- strict-subset factors reproduce a hyperfactor;
- shuffled/wrong factors work equally well;
- the gain is solely uncharged side information or extra observations;
- exact flat and factorized forms have the same result and no representation or
  computation advantage remains;
- a standard graphical decoder matches or beats the orbit-specific method; or
- approximate gains disappear at equal operations, bytes, and calibration.

## 7. `PRW-G2A` — adaptive factor/hyperedge acquisition

`PRW-G2A` acquires new evidence from one allowed node, edge, or hyperedge. It is
distinct from scheduling which already-known message to compute next.

The null is that exact MaxEJS/Chernoff, mutual-information greedy, best static,
or random acquisition matches the orbit-aware policy on the same actions and
costs. The alternative requires a strict expected-cost/error Pareto advantage,
or approximate-MaxEJS quality with materially lower policy-evaluation work.

If no new data are acquired, the experiment is classified separately as
`PRW-G2S` adaptive computation scheduling. Its controls are residual/prioritized
belief propagation, prioritized sweeping, beam search, and exact inference. It
cannot inherit evidence from `PRW-G2A`.

Adaptive factor selection is not evidence for `PRW-Q1`, `PRW-L1`, queue-aware
routing, or living memory.
