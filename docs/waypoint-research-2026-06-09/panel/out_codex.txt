## 1. Mathematical audit

### Encoder upgrades are not affine “by construction”

Let \(x=f_{\text{old}}(t)\) and \(y=P f_{\text{new}}(t)\), where \(P\) is the fixed projection. A global affine model assumes

\[
y = Ax+b+\epsilon.
\]

If this equation is true and enough full-rank paired samples are available, then affine least squares is optimal under squared loss. But the equation is an empirical hypothesis, not a consequence of using two frozen transformers.

Both encoders are nonlinear functions of text. The fact that \(P\) is linear only says that the final projection is linear in the new embedding; it does not make \(P f_{\text{new}}\) affine in \(f_{\text{old}}\).

The Procrustes theorem is also being used backwards. It establishes, roughly:

\[
X^\top X \approx Y^\top Y
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists Q\text{ orthogonal such that }QX\approx Y.
\]

It does not establish that independently trained encoders must have approximately equal Gram matrices. Dot-product preservation is the assumption, not the conclusion. The paper itself evaluates that assumption empirically and uses 10,000 paired texts to estimate each transform. [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)

Indeed, Drift-Adapter found that its residual MLP consistently beat linear alternatives in its ordinary MiniLM→MPNet setting, and in severe GloVe→MPNet drift obtained ARR 0.715 versus 0.587 for Procrustes and 0.632 for low-rank affine. That is direct counterevidence to the universal “encoder upgrades are essentially affine” formulation. [Drift-Adapter](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/emnlp/2025.emnlp-main.805.pdf)

The defensible claim is narrower:

> On the tested ChelatedAI datasets, after the particular projection and with the available supervision, regularized affine regression captured most of the recoverable retrieval performance.

That is useful. It is not a structural theorem.

### What the ridge result actually shows

Ridge recovering 84.4% while unregularized least squares recovers 67% demonstrates substantial finite-sample variance, collinearity, or conditioning sensitivity. It is not especially strong evidence that the population map is exactly linear.

If the true noiseless relation were a well-conditioned affine map and \(n\gg d\), unregularized least squares would normally recover it. Ridge’s large advantage means that sample complexity and effective dimensionality are material parts of the result.

Likewise, one residual MLP scoring 81% does not establish a nonlinear ceiling because its result depends on:

- architecture and residual parameterization;
- anchor count and coverage;
- regularization and early stopping;
- whether MSE is aligned with NDCG;
- the projection seed;
- optimization quality;
- query-level statistical uncertainty.

On the canonical SciFact numbers, ridge and MLP correspond to approximately

\[
0.844(0.805)=0.679,\qquad
0.81(0.805)=0.652.
\]

The absolute NDCG difference is only about 0.027. Without paired query bootstrap intervals or repeated projection/anchor splits, one cannot even quantify how firmly the ordering is established.

### The “84% linear ceiling” is not valid

It is legitimate to call 84% the **best observed linear recovery** or an **empirical plateau under the tested protocol**. “Ceiling” requires substantially more:

1. A learning curve showing ridge has converged with anchor count.
2. Multiple high-capacity alternatives selected without test leakage.
3. Repeated anchor, model-training, and projection seeds.
4. Held-out embedding reconstruction as well as retrieval.
5. Confidence intervals tight enough to exclude a consequential nonlinear gain.
6. Ideally, a bound connecting embedding residuals to ranking margins.

No Procrustes bound implies an 84% NDCG ceiling. Alignment error and NDCG are different quantities. Ranking is controlled by query-specific margins. If oracle document \(r\) beats competitor \(j\) by margin

\[
m=q^\top y_r-q^\top y_j,
\]

then mapped errors \(e_r,e_j\) preserve that pair only when, for example,

\[
m > \lVert q\rVert(\lVert e_r\rVert+\lVert e_j\rVert).
\]

Thus the same Frobenius alignment error can yield very different NDCG depending on where errors occur relative to small top-\(k\) margins.

There may also be a genuine information ceiling, but it is not necessarily linear. If the old embedding has discarded distinctions represented by the new encoder, then \(\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)>0\), and no deterministic point map \(g(X)\)—linear or nonlinear—can reconstruct them. A graph can add corpus context, but cannot reliably recreate semantic information absent from both the old points and their old-space relationships.

### Why the near-identity method loses

Here the “wrong arena” defense is valid.

If the constraint is

\[
\lVert g(x)-x\rVert\leq\alpha\lVert x\rVert
\]

and unit vectors require a rotation through angle \(\theta\), then displacement is \(2\sin(\theta/2)\). With \(\alpha=0.05\), the largest permitted angle is only about \(2.86^\circ\). A substantial rotation is therefore outside the hypothesis class.

There is an even deeper mismatch: an orthogonal global rotation preserves the document cloud’s Gram matrix and topology. A detector looking only at document-side topology cannot observe that the query space has rotated. Without paired anchors, query observations, or another external reference, the incompatibility is unidentifiable.

So the upgrade benchmark is adversarial to chelation’s design, but not unfair. It asks a real operational question and exposes that chelation lacks both the range and information required for encoder migration.

### Local maps: sound hypothesis, serious overfitting risk

Suppose the true map is piecewise affine:

\[
Y=A_cX+b_c+\epsilon,\qquad c=c(X).
\]

Then local maps can reduce global-model approximation bias. But their test error adds three costs:

\[
\text{risk}
=
\text{approximation bias}
+\text{estimation variance}
+\text{routing error}.
\]

A full \(d\)-dimensional affine map has \(d(d+1)\) coefficients. \(K\) independent maps multiply that count by \(K\). With \(d=384\), this is approximately 148,000 coefficients globally and 2.36 million at \(K=16\). Each output regression also becomes dependent on only about \(n/K\) examples.

The main hazards are:

- unsupervised clusters may reflect topic, length, or density rather than mapping residuals;
- hard routing creates discontinuities at cluster boundaries;
- sparse and rare-entity clusters have the least data precisely where complexity is supposedly needed;
- choosing \(K\) on retrieval test results produces large selection bias;
- unrestricted local maps are not a fair comparison with one regularized global map.

A shared global map plus shrinkage-controlled low-rank local residuals is much more credible than independent full maps.

## 2. The single decisive experiment

Run one preregistered **regime × corrector crossover** on the pinned BEIR Quora collection: 522,931 documents, the official 5,000-query development split for selection, and the 10,000-query test split for confirmation. These sample sizes are documented by the BEIR releases. [BEIR datasets](https://github.com/beir-cellar/beir)

### Data and repetitions

Use MiniLM-L6 as the 384-dimensional old encoder and MPNet-base as the new encoder.

For each of five predetermined seeds:

- Create one Gaussian 768→384 projection with orthonormalized rows.
- Exclude every qrel-positive document from the anchor pool.
- Draw 100,000 distinct calibration documents:
  - 64,000 training pairs;
  - 16,000 validation pairs;
  - 20,000 untouched reconstruction-test pairs.
- Never substitute known target embeddings directly into the retrieval index; all documents must pass through the learned function.
- Use exact cosine retrieval over the full corpus.
- Freeze every hyperparameter after the 5,000 development queries; touch the 10,000 test queries once.

### Regime U: real encoder upgrade

Documents contain old MiniLM vectors. Queries and oracle documents use projected MPNet vectors.

Compare:

1. Affine ridge.
2. Orthogonal Procrustes with centering.
3. Residual MLP, widths 256, 512, and 1,024.
4. Independent per-cluster ridge maps with \(K\in\{2,4,8,16\}\).
5. Parameter-controlled local model:
   \[
   g_c(x)=Ax+b+U_cV_c^\top x,
   \]
   with rank 8 local residuals and soft routing.
6. Graph residual regression: fit the global map, then kernel-smooth anchor residuals over old-space \(k\)-NN with \(k\in\{16,64,256\}\).
7. The existing bounded chelation corrector at \(\alpha=0.05\).

Clusters must be fitted from training old vectors only. Validation chooses ridge penalties, \(K\), MLP, and graph hyperparameters by a preregistered combination of reconstruction MSE and development NDCG.

Report NDCG@10, recovery, held-out cosine error, reconstruction MSE, per-cluster sample count, boundary-query performance, and effective parameter count.

### Regime C: favorable small heterogeneous collapse

This is a mechanistic screen of chelation’s claimed home turf, not evidence that the pathology occurs naturally.

Fit \(K=16\) clusters in the old MiniLM space. For eight randomly selected clusters, collapse document vectors toward their cluster centroid:

\[
x_i'=
\operatorname{normalize}\left((1-\beta)x_i+\beta\mu_{c(i)}\right),
\qquad
\beta\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}.
\]

Queries stay in the uncorrupted MiniLM space. The clean index is the evaluation oracle but is unavailable to unsupervised methods.

Compare:

- identity/corrupted index;
- global centering and ZCA whitening;
- all-but-the-top;
- cluster-based isotropy enhancement;
- local and global hubness scaling;
- bounded chelation with its topology/isomer detector;
- paired global and local regression only as privileged diagnostic oracles.

This is necessary because local cluster correction and hubness mitigation are already established baselines, not new territory. Cluster-based isotropy correction dates at least to Rajaee and Pilehvar, while local/global scaling for hubness dates to 2012. [Cluster-based isotropy enhancement](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-short.73.pdf), [local and global hubness scaling](https://jmlr.org/beta/papers/v13/schnitzer12a.html)

Define recovered induced loss as

\[
R_C=
\frac{\operatorname{NDCG}(m)-\operatorname{NDCG}(\text{corrupt})}
{\operatorname{NDCG}(\text{clean})-\operatorname{NDCG}(\text{corrupt})}.
\]

### Statistical decision rule

Use 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples of test queries, hierarchical over the five seeds. Apply Holm correction to comparisons with global ridge.

A meaningful win is preregistered as:

- at least **five recovery points**;
- at least **0.02 absolute NDCG@10**;
- 95% simultaneous CI excluding zero;
- same sign in all five seeds;
- at least 10% lower held-out reconstruction MSE for paired methods.

### Predicted outcomes under each hypothesis

| Hypothesis | Confirmatory prediction |
|---|---|
| Upgrade relation is effectively global affine | Ridge recovery 82–87%; every MLP/local/graph model within ±2 points; nonlinear held-out MSE improvement <5%. |
| Real upgrade drift is piecewise local | Cross-fitted local model ≥90% recovery, ≥5 points over ridge, and ≥10% lower reconstruction MSE; gain persists at cluster boundaries and across all seeds. |
| Local maps merely overfit | Training recovery ≥95%, but test gain ≤1 point or negative; seed SD ≥4 points; smallest clusters and boundaries degrade most; selected \(K\) falls toward 1–2 under stronger regularization. |
| The old kNN graph contains missing target information | Graph residual model beats ridge by ≥5 points and the best pointwise local map by ≥2 points. A ≤2-point gain means it is acting only as a regularizer. |
| Chelation was genuinely mis-benchmarked | Chelation remains ≤5% in Regime U but recovers ≥60% of induced loss in Regime C, beats every unsupervised baseline by ≥10 recovery points, and detects corrupted clusters with AUPRC ≥0.80. |
| The reframe is rationalization | Chelation recovers <20% in Regime C, fails to beat cluster isotropy/local scaling, or detector AUPRC ≤0.60. |
| 84% is only an observed plateau | Some flexible method reaches ≥89% with corrected CI excluding ridge. |
| 84% is a robust empirical plateau here | All methods stay within two points of ridge as anchors rise from 1,000→4,000→16,000→64,000. Even then, call it a Quora/model-pair plateau—not a universal ceiling. |

My forecast is ridge 82–86%, local/MLP/graph between roughly 81–88%, and no stable five-point local gain. For Regime C, I expect chelation to recover only 10–35% of induced loss and established local-scaling/cluster methods to recover about 20–45%. I assign only a 10–15% probability to the decisive chelation crossover.

## 3. Ranking P1–P4

1. **P4 plus P3: real longitudinal fixed-encoder corpus shift.** This is the only direction that could distinguish the project, but it must be reformulated. Frozen vectors do not deteriorate merely by sitting in a store. “Growth” must mean changing corpus mixture, duplication, domain/language influx, or density—not spontaneous embedding drift. Chance that the current chelation repair beats strong baselines by ≥0.02 NDCG across three real datasets: **10–15%**. Groundbreaking potential: **5–8%**.

2. **P3: detection-gated, anchor-efficient routing.** Plausible as an engineering result if the detector predicts actual retrieval loss rather than geometric oddity. Chance of matching full-anchor performance with ≤10% of anchors: **25–35%**. Groundbreaking: **~5%**.

3. **P2: graph residual correction.** Technically sound with paired residual anchors, but graph smoothing, manifold regularization, and neighborhood-based hubness correction are mature ideas. Chance of beating ridge by five recovery points: **~15%**. Groundbreaking: **2–3%**.

4. **P1: local paired maps.** Fastest falsifier, lowest novelty. Drift-Adapter has already shown a synthetic metadata-routed version, and cluster-local representation correction predates this project. Chance of a stable five-point gain under fair routing and regularization: **~20%**. Groundbreaking: **~1%**.

A stronger missed direction is a **recoverability certificate**: estimate paired Gram distortion and query margin distributions, then predict before deployment whether an affine adapter will recover 70%, 90%, or 99% of retrieval. A theorem or calibrated estimator connecting alignment residuals to top-\(k\) ranking regret would build naturally on Procrustes theory and the project’s unusually severe benchmark. I give that direction about a **25% chance of an exceptional paper and 5–10% of a genuinely important result**, though it is no longer chelation.

## Answers to the three questions

### 1. Revival

The current mechanism is unlikely to be revived into a groundbreaking general correction method. The strongest revival is a rigorous negative/methodology paper plus a recoverability analysis.

My odds:

- valid publishable methodology paper: **65–75%**;
- exceptional paper: **20–25%**;
- groundbreaking chelation mechanism: **<5%**.

### 2. Falsification

Yes: there is enough evidence to end the **bounded, unpaired chelation-for-encoder-upgrades** hypothesis. Correct paired regression dominates, the bound excludes the necessary motion, and document-only topology cannot identify an orientation mismatch.

No: the experiments do not falsify heterogeneous within-model correction because they did not test it. But that hypothesis should be labeled **unsupported**, not “surviving evidence.” Unpaired geometric repair is underidentified: multiple transformations can improve isotropy or topology without preserving semantics, a standard problem in unpaired translation. [Identifiability in unsupervised domain translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09671)

Moreover, isotropy improvement is not reliably equivalent to semantic or retrieval improvement. Prior studies found inconsistent gains and, in one broad comparison, essentially no significant relationship between isotropy and representation quality. [Is Anisotropy Truly Harmful?](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-short.103.pdf), [On Isotropy Calibration](https://aclanthology.org/2022.insights-1.1/)

- Until then, the honest verdict is: **encoder-upgrade chelation is dead; within-model chelation is scientifically open but evidentially near-dead, with one decisive test remaining.**

- The encoder-upgrade results strongly falsify **bounded, near-identity, unpaired chelation as a competitive encoder-migration method**. I would stop pursuing that application with roughly **95% confidence**.
- They do **not** logically falsify correction of small, heterogeneous, within-model pathologies, because that regime was not tested.
- But the within-model hypothesis is not thereby “alive” in an evidentiary sense. It is **untested, poorly identified, heavily covered by prior work, and currently low probability—about 10–15% for a meaningful win by this corrector**.
- The reported 84% is an empirical score from one model class and setup, not a mathematical or demonstrated statistical ceiling.
- P1’s basic hypothesis has already appeared in Drift-Adapter: its appendix reports metadata-routed local adapters improving synthetic heterogeneous-drift ARR from 0.85 to 0.94. The fair unsupervised-clustering version remains worth testing, but it would be incremental rather than groundbreaking. [Drift-Adapter](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/emnlp/2025.emnlp-main.805.pdf)

## 1. Mathematical audit

### Encoder upgrades are not affine “by construction”

Let \(x=f_{\text{old}}(t)\) and \(y=P f_{\text{new}}(t)\), where \(P\) is the fixed projection. A global affine model assumes

\[
y = Ax+b+\epsilon.
\]

If this equation is true and enough full-rank paired samples are available, then affine least squares is optimal under squared loss. But the equation is an empirical hypothesis, not a consequence of using two frozen transformers.

Both encoders are nonlinear functions of text. The fact that \(P\) is linear only says that the final projection is linear in the new embedding; it does not make \(P f_{\text{new}}\) affine in \(f_{\text{old}}\).

The Procrustes theorem is also being used backwards. It establishes, roughly:

\[
tokens used
131,671
X^\top X \approx Y^\top Y
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists Q\text{ orthogonal such that }QX\approx Y.
\]

It does not establish that independently trained encoders must have approximately equal Gram matrices. Dot-product preservation is the assumption, not the conclusion. The paper itself evaluates that assumption empirically and uses 10,000 paired texts to estimate each transform. [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)

Indeed, Drift-Adapter found that its residual MLP consistently beat linear alternatives in its ordinary MiniLM→MPNet setting, and in severe GloVe→MPNet drift obtained ARR 0.715 versus 0.587 for Procrustes and 0.632 for low-rank affine. That is direct counterevidence to the universal “encoder upgrades are essentially affine” formulation. [Drift-Adapter](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/emnlp/2025.emnlp-main.805.pdf)

The defensible claim is narrower:

> On the tested ChelatedAI datasets, after the particular projection and with the available supervision, regularized affine regression captured most of the recoverable retrieval performance.

That is useful. It is not a structural theorem.

### What the ridge result actually shows

Ridge recovering 84.4% while unregularized least squares recovers 67% demonstrates substantial finite-sample variance, collinearity, or conditioning sensitivity. It is not especially strong evidence that the population map is exactly linear.

If the true noiseless relation were a well-conditioned affine map and \(n\gg d\), unregularized least squares would normally recover it. Ridge’s large advantage means that sample complexity and effective dimensionality are material parts of the result.

Likewise, one residual MLP scoring 81% does not establish a nonlinear ceiling because its result depends on:

- architecture and residual parameterization;
- anchor count and coverage;
- regularization and early stopping;
- whether MSE is aligned with NDCG;
- the projection seed;
- optimization quality;
- query-level statistical uncertainty.

On the canonical SciFact numbers, ridge and MLP correspond to approximately

\[
0.844(0.805)=0.679,\qquad
0.81(0.805)=0.652.
\]

The absolute NDCG difference is only about 0.027. Without paired query bootstrap intervals or repeated projection/anchor splits, one cannot even quantify how firmly the ordering is established.

### The “84% linear ceiling” is not valid

It is legitimate to call 84% the **best observed linear recovery** or an **empirical plateau under the tested protocol**. “Ceiling” requires substantially more:

1. A learning curve showing ridge has converged with anchor count.
2. Multiple high-capacity alternatives selected without test leakage.
3. Repeated anchor, model-training, and projection seeds.
4. Held-out embedding reconstruction as well as retrieval.
5. Confidence intervals tight enough to exclude a consequential nonlinear gain.
6. Ideally, a bound connecting embedding residuals to ranking margins.

No Procrustes bound implies an 84% NDCG ceiling. Alignment error and NDCG are different quantities. Ranking is controlled by query-specific margins. If oracle document \(r\) beats competitor \(j\) by margin

\[
m=q^\top y_r-q^\top y_j,
\]

then mapped errors \(e_r,e_j\) preserve that pair only when, for example,

\[
m > \lVert q\rVert(\lVert e_r\rVert+\lVert e_j\rVert).
\]

Thus the same Frobenius alignment error can yield very different NDCG depending on where errors occur relative to small top-\(k\) margins.

There may also be a genuine information ceiling, but it is not necessarily linear. If the old embedding has discarded distinctions represented by the new encoder, then \(\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)>0\), and no deterministic point map \(g(X)\)—linear or nonlinear—can reconstruct them. A graph can add corpus context, but cannot reliably recreate semantic information absent from both the old points and their old-space relationships.

### Why the near-identity method loses

Here the “wrong arena” defense is valid.

If the constraint is

\[
\lVert g(x)-x\rVert\leq\alpha\lVert x\rVert
\]

and unit vectors require a rotation through angle \(\theta\), then displacement is \(2\sin(\theta/2)\). With \(\alpha=0.05\), the largest permitted angle is only about \(2.86^\circ\). A substantial rotation is therefore outside the hypothesis class.

There is an even deeper mismatch: an orthogonal global rotation preserves the document cloud’s Gram matrix and topology. A detector looking only at document-side topology cannot observe that the query space has rotated. Without paired anchors, query observations, or another external reference, the incompatibility is unidentifiable.

So the upgrade benchmark is adversarial to chelation’s design, but not unfair. It asks a real operational question and exposes that chelation lacks both the range and information required for encoder migration.

### Local maps: sound hypothesis, serious overfitting risk

Suppose the true map is piecewise affine:

\[
Y=A_cX+b_c+\epsilon,\qquad c=c(X).
\]

Then local maps can reduce global-model approximation bias. But their test error adds three costs:

\[
\text{risk}
=
\text{approximation bias}
+\text{estimation variance}
+\text{routing error}.
\]

A full \(d\)-dimensional affine map has \(d(d+1)\) coefficients. \(K\) independent maps multiply that count by \(K\). With \(d=384\), this is approximately 148,000 coefficients globally and 2.36 million at \(K=16\). Each output regression also becomes dependent on only about \(n/K\) examples.

The main hazards are:

- unsupervised clusters may reflect topic, length, or density rather than mapping residuals;
- hard routing creates discontinuities at cluster boundaries;
- sparse and rare-entity clusters have the least data precisely where complexity is supposedly needed;
- choosing \(K\) on retrieval test results produces large selection bias;
- unrestricted local maps are not a fair comparison with one regularized global map.

A shared global map plus shrinkage-controlled low-rank local residuals is much more credible than independent full maps.

## 2. The single decisive experiment

Run one preregistered **regime × corrector crossover** on the pinned BEIR Quora collection: 522,931 documents, the official 5,000-query development split for selection, and the 10,000-query test split for confirmation. These sample sizes are documented by the BEIR releases. [BEIR datasets](https://github.com/beir-cellar/beir)

### Data and repetitions

Use MiniLM-L6 as the 384-dimensional old encoder and MPNet-base as the new encoder.

For each of five predetermined seeds:

- Create one Gaussian 768→384 projection with orthonormalized rows.
- Exclude every qrel-positive document from the anchor pool.
- Draw 100,000 distinct calibration documents:
  - 64,000 training pairs;
  - 16,000 validation pairs;
  - 20,000 untouched reconstruction-test pairs.
- Never substitute known target embeddings directly into the retrieval index; all documents must pass through the learned function.
- Use exact cosine retrieval over the full corpus.
- Freeze every hyperparameter after the 5,000 development queries; touch the 10,000 test queries once.

### Regime U: real encoder upgrade

Documents contain old MiniLM vectors. Queries and oracle documents use projected MPNet vectors.

Compare:

1. Affine ridge.
2. Orthogonal Procrustes with centering.
3. Residual MLP, widths 256, 512, and 1,024.
4. Independent per-cluster ridge maps with \(K\in\{2,4,8,16\}\).
5. Parameter-controlled local model:
   \[
   g_c(x)=Ax+b+U_cV_c^\top x,
   \]
   with rank 8 local residuals and soft routing.
6. Graph residual regression: fit the global map, then kernel-smooth anchor residuals over old-space \(k\)-NN with \(k\in\{16,64,256\}\).
7. The existing bounded chelation corrector at \(\alpha=0.05\).

Clusters must be fitted from training old vectors only. Validation chooses ridge penalties, \(K\), MLP, and graph hyperparameters by a preregistered combination of reconstruction MSE and development NDCG.

Report NDCG@10, recovery, held-out cosine error, reconstruction MSE, per-cluster sample count, boundary-query performance, and effective parameter count.

### Regime C: favorable small heterogeneous collapse

This is a mechanistic screen of chelation’s claimed home turf, not evidence that the pathology occurs naturally.

Fit \(K=16\) clusters in the old MiniLM space. For eight randomly selected clusters, collapse document vectors toward their cluster centroid:

\[
x_i'=
\operatorname{normalize}\left((1-\beta)x_i+\beta\mu_{c(i)}\right),
\qquad
\beta\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}.
\]

Queries stay in the uncorrupted MiniLM space. The clean index is the evaluation oracle but is unavailable to unsupervised methods.

Compare:

- identity/corrupted index;
- global centering and ZCA whitening;
- all-but-the-top;
- cluster-based isotropy enhancement;
- local and global hubness scaling;
- bounded chelation with its topology/isomer detector;
- paired global and local regression only as privileged diagnostic oracles.

This is necessary because local cluster correction and hubness mitigation are already established baselines, not new territory. Cluster-based isotropy correction dates at least to Rajaee and Pilehvar, while local/global scaling for hubness dates to 2012. [Cluster-based isotropy enhancement](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-short.73.pdf), [local and global hubness scaling](https://jmlr.org/beta/papers/v13/schnitzer12a.html)

Define recovered induced loss as

\[
R_C=
\frac{\operatorname{NDCG}(m)-\operatorname{NDCG}(\text{corrupt})}
{\operatorname{NDCG}(\text{clean})-\operatorname{NDCG}(\text{corrupt})}.
\]

### Statistical decision rule

Use 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples of test queries, hierarchical over the five seeds. Apply Holm correction to comparisons with global ridge.

A meaningful win is preregistered as:

- at least **five recovery points**;
- at least **0.02 absolute NDCG@10**;
- 95% simultaneous CI excluding zero;
- same sign in all five seeds;
- at least 10% lower held-out reconstruction MSE for paired methods.

### Predicted outcomes under each hypothesis

| Hypothesis | Confirmatory prediction |
|---|---|
| Upgrade relation is effectively global affine | Ridge recovery 82–87%; every MLP/local/graph model within ±2 points; nonlinear held-out MSE improvement <5%. |
| Real upgrade drift is piecewise local | Cross-fitted local model ≥90% recovery, ≥5 points over ridge, and ≥10% lower reconstruction MSE; gain persists at cluster boundaries and across all seeds. |
| Local maps merely overfit | Training recovery ≥95%, but test gain ≤1 point or negative; seed SD ≥4 points; smallest clusters and boundaries degrade most; selected \(K\) falls toward 1–2 under stronger regularization. |
| The old kNN graph contains missing target information | Graph residual model beats ridge by ≥5 points and the best pointwise local map by ≥2 points. A ≤2-point gain means it is acting only as a regularizer. |
| Chelation was genuinely mis-benchmarked | Chelation remains ≤5% in Regime U but recovers ≥60% of induced loss in Regime C, beats every unsupervised baseline by ≥10 recovery points, and detects corrupted clusters with AUPRC ≥0.80. |
| The reframe is rationalization | Chelation recovers <20% in Regime C, fails to beat cluster isotropy/local scaling, or detector AUPRC ≤0.60. |
| 84% is only an observed plateau | Some flexible method reaches ≥89% with corrected CI excluding ridge. |
| 84% is a robust empirical plateau here | All methods stay within two points of ridge as anchors rise from 1,000→4,000→16,000→64,000. Even then, call it a Quora/model-pair plateau—not a universal ceiling. |

My forecast is ridge 82–86%, local/MLP/graph between roughly 81–88%, and no stable five-point local gain. For Regime C, I expect chelation to recover only 10–35% of induced loss and established local-scaling/cluster methods to recover about 20–45%. I assign only a 10–15% probability to the decisive chelation crossover.

## 3. Ranking P1–P4

1. **P4 plus P3: real longitudinal fixed-encoder corpus shift.** This is the only direction that could distinguish the project, but it must be reformulated. Frozen vectors do not deteriorate merely by sitting in a store. “Growth” must mean changing corpus mixture, duplication, domain/language influx, or density—not spontaneous embedding drift. Chance that the current chelation repair beats strong baselines by ≥0.02 NDCG across three real datasets: **10–15%**. Groundbreaking potential: **5–8%**.

2. **P3: detection-gated, anchor-efficient routing.** Plausible as an engineering result if the detector predicts actual retrieval loss rather than geometric oddity. Chance of matching full-anchor performance with ≤10% of anchors: **25–35%**. Groundbreaking: **~5%**.

3. **P2: graph residual correction.** Technically sound with paired residual anchors, but graph smoothing, manifold regularization, and neighborhood-based hubness correction are mature ideas. Chance of beating ridge by five recovery points: **~15%**. Groundbreaking: **2–3%**.

4. **P1: local paired maps.** Fastest falsifier, lowest novelty. Drift-Adapter has already shown a synthetic metadata-routed version, and cluster-local representation correction predates this project. Chance of a stable five-point gain under fair routing and regularization: **~20%**. Groundbreaking: **~1%**.

A stronger missed direction is a **recoverability certificate**: estimate paired Gram distortion and query margin distributions, then predict before deployment whether an affine adapter will recover 70%, 90%, or 99% of retrieval. A theorem or calibrated estimator connecting alignment residuals to top-\(k\) ranking regret would build naturally on Procrustes theory and the project’s unusually severe benchmark. I give that direction about a **25% chance of an exceptional paper and 5–10% of a genuinely important result**, though it is no longer chelation.

## Answers to the three questions

### 1. Revival

The current mechanism is unlikely to be revived into a groundbreaking general correction method. The strongest revival is a rigorous negative/methodology paper plus a recoverability analysis.

My odds:

- valid publishable methodology paper: **65–75%**;
- exceptional paper: **20–25%**;
- groundbreaking chelation mechanism: **<5%**.

### 2. Falsification

Yes: there is enough evidence to end the **bounded, unpaired chelation-for-encoder-upgrades** hypothesis. Correct paired regression dominates, the bound excludes the necessary motion, and document-only topology cannot identify an orientation mismatch.

No: the experiments do not falsify heterogeneous within-model correction because they did not test it. But that hypothesis should be labeled **unsupported**, not “surviving evidence.” Unpaired geometric repair is underidentified: multiple transformations can improve isotropy or topology without preserving semantics, a standard problem in unpaired translation. [Identifiability in unsupervised domain translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09671)

Moreover, isotropy improvement is not reliably equivalent to semantic or retrieval improvement. Prior studies found inconsistent gains and, in one broad comparison, essentially no significant relationship between isotropy and representation quality. [Is Anisotropy Truly Harmful?](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-short.103.pdf), [On Isotropy Calibration](https://aclanthology.org/2022.insights-1.1/)

### 3. Path forward

Do not run another unconstrained corrector sweep on SciFact. Run the preregistered crossover above.

- If chelation cannot beat CBIE/local scaling in the favorable synthetic local-collapse arm, terminate the correction mechanism in both regimes.
- If it passes there but not on a real longitudinal corpus-growth benchmark, retain it only as a synthetic capability result.
- If it produces the predicted crossover and then repeats on at least three natural evolving stores, the “mis-benchmarked” defense becomes evidence-backed.
- Until then, the honest verdict is: **encoder-upgrade chelation is dead; within-model chelation is scientifically open but evidentially near-dead, with one decisive test remaining.**
