## Executive Summary

This report delivers a **deep, grounded exploration** of loss functions and training objectives that fuse **chelation-style residual correction** (lightweight `x + δ(x)` adapters with near-identity init, L2 normalization, BoundedAdapter, Procrustes/LowRank variants) with the core ideas from **On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD)**, **Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO)**, **MIS-PO filtered online RL**, and related 2025–2026 embedding self-distillation work (MoSE hierarchical encoder distillation, AsymDSD, ATAS any-to-any).

**Core Mapping (the key insight)**:
- **Student policy/view**: `ChelationAdapter.forward(x)` under normal inference context (no extra diagnostics).
- **Teacher / privileged view**: Target computation or re-evaluation conditioned on **rich feedback** = `SelfEditDirective` (synthetic_examples, optimization_params, strategy), `diagnostics` dict (structural_health, collisions from chelation_log), `CandidateLedgerEntry`, `fitness`, `quantization_gate` results, provenance, homeostatic targets from sedimentation.
- **On-policy "rollouts"**: Current adapter outputs (or deltas) on live queries/docs or synthetic probes generated by `SelfHealingChelationPlanner`.
- **"Divergence" in vector space**: Not token logits, but embedding similarities (cosine softmax matrices), vector distances (MSE/cosine on deltas or final embs), or proxy log-probs derived from exp(-dist) or sim scores.
- **Stabilizers from literature**: Per-"token"/per-sample or per-dimension pointwise clipping of divergence contribs (prevents "stylistic" high-variance dims or stylistic tokens dominating); EMA or trust-region teacher; binary ratio filtering (MIS-PO style on fitness_delta, delta_norm, retention_score); KL retention to base embeddings or zero-delta adapter (prevents catastrophic forgetting).
- **Why this solves CHELATEDAI pain points** (from audit + panel reviews): Current sedimentation (MSE/InfoNCE/Hybrid) is mostly off-policy or weakly on-policy, lacks dense privileged self-supervision from directives, has high gradient variance, no explicit KL control for stability, and self-healing plans never get reinforced persistently in a sample-efficient way. OPSD/SDPO-style objectives provide **dense, on-policy, self-generated supervision** with built-in retention and filtering — exactly the "missing practical layer" for making chelation production-viable.

**8 Concrete Candidate Objectives** are proposed (spanning basic to advanced, all implementable on top of existing `ChelationAdapter.regularization_loss()`, `BoundedAdapter`, `SedimentationInfoNCELoss`, `online_updater.OnlineLossFunction`, and `SelfHealingChelationPlanner`).

Each includes:
- Mathematical form
- Full pseudocode (ready-to-implement PyTorch)
- Theoretical properties (gradient behavior, convexity/regularization effects, information geometry)
- Expected training dynamics (convergence speed like OPSD's ~100-step rapid gains, sample efficiency 4-8x over pure RL-style, forgetting risk)
- Direct applicability to ChelationAdapter family + SelfEditDirectives
- Pros / Cons + risks + mitigations

**Key Practical Recommendations**:
- New pluggable `ChelationSelfDistillLoss` base (extends `OnlineLossFunction` and sedimentation losses).
- Factory `create_chelation_distill_loss(loss_type="opsd_asymmetric", directive=..., diagnostics=..., teacher_mode="ema|fixed|privileged_target")`.
- Gradients **only through student adapter** (stopgrad on teacher targets/views).
- Teacher is either (a) fixed initial adapter snapshot, (b) slow EMA of student, or (c) non-parametric privileged target generator from directive + chelation_log.
- Combine with existing `BoundedAdapter` (min/max correction norms for INT8 survival) and Procrustes reg.
- Integration points: `antigravity_engine.run_sedimentation_cycle`, `self_healing_chelation.SelfEditSandboxExecutor`, `online_updater`, `model_scope_trainer`.
- For testing: Extend `test_sedimentation_loss.py` + new `test_chelation_self_distill_losses.py`; use synthetic `SelfEditDirective` + mock diagnostics; metrics from `RetrievalFitnessEvaluator` + retention (NDCG@10 delta), stability (adapter drift norm), quant survival gate.

These designs directly address documented CRITICAL issues (F-ML-001–047, JO-/PS-/RT- findings, instability in multi-cycle sedimentation, poor self-edit reinforcement, forgetting on Procrustes/MLP adapters).

## 1. Deep Research Synthesis: Relevant Literature & Techniques (Digested for Chelation)

### 1.1 OPSD (Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation, arXiv:2601.18734, Zhao et al. 2026)
- **Core**: Single LLM = student (pS(.|x)) + teacher (pT(.|x, y*)). Student samples on-policy ŷ ~ pS(.|x). Minimize per-token D(pT(.|x,y*,ŷ<n) || pS(.|x,ŷ<n)) averaged over |ŷ|. D = forward KL / reverse KL / JSD_β.
- **Stabilizers**: (a) Pointwise per-vocab-entry clipping: min(ℓ_{n,v}^{(f)}, τ) before sum (prevents stylistic tokens like "therefore" dominating math tokens). (b) Teacher fixed as initial policy (implicit retention). (c) Sampled-token variant as dense-reward policy gradient: advantage r_n = log pT(ŷn | ...) - log pS(...) , then standard REINFORCE-style.
- **Dynamics**: Rapid convergence (strong gains in <100–few hundred steps), 4–8× token efficiency vs GRPO, dense credit assignment even on incorrect rollouts (unlike STaR which filters hard).
- **Relevance to embeddings**: "x" = input text, "y*" = privileged diagnostic + ground-truth retrieval target or SelfEditDirective, "tokens" → embedding dimensions or in-batch similarity "positions", "logits" → normalized embedding or softmax(sim matrix). "Rationalization" → directive + diagnostics explaining *why* a collision/poor fitness occurred.

### 1.2 SDPO (Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation, arXiv:2601.20802, Hübotter et al. 2026)
- **Core**: Sample rollout y ~ π_θ(.|x), obtain rich tokenized feedback f (errors, logs, judge comments). Self-teacher = same model conditioned on (x, f). Distill via KL( student_logits | stopgrad(teacher_logits on (x,f,y<t)) ). Works with scalar rewards by reusing successful y as implicit f for failures on same x.
- **Config from impl (verl/SDPO repo)**: `loss_mode=sdpo`, `alpha` (0=forward KL teacher||student, 1=reverse, 0.5=JSD), `full_logit_distillation` vs `distillation_topk`, `teacher_regularization=ema|trust-region`, `is_clip` for importance ratio, EMA update_rate=0.05.
- **Dynamics**: 4–6× sample efficiency, shorter traces (11×), works at test-time for iterative self-correction, scales with model size (better in-context retrospection).
- **Mapping**: "f" = rich chelation diagnostics + SelfEditDirective + ledger entry + quant gate verdict. "Rich feedback" construction is the **killer app** for `self_healing_chelation.py` — directives already generate synthetic_examples and optimization_params precisely for this.

### 1.3 MIS-PO (Metropolis Independence Sampling – Filtered Policy Optimization, ~arXiv 2602.10604 + ar0cket1 Hermes-Agent-Online-RL)
- **Core**: For single-trajectory (no group) online RL/LoRA self-improvement from human/text feedback. Instead of continuous importance weights (high variance), use **binary filtering** at token + trajectory level based on prob ratio bounds (e.g. [0.8, 1.25] token, [0.9, 1.1] traj). + small KL penalty to ref policy. Extremely stable for continual adapter updates.
- **Relevance**: Perfect for chelation's "accept/reject directive" + BoundedAdapter. Filter training examples where "ratio" analog (fitness gain / delta_norm / retention_score / quant_survival) is in safe band. Dramatically lowers variance in sedimentation cycles.

### 1.4 Embedding-Specific Self-Distillation (MoSE, AsymDSD, ATAS, Hierarchical Contrastive + SD)
- **MoSE (ModularStarEncoder, arXiv ~2503.03008)**: Multi-exit encoder; higher layers self-distill to early/intermediate layers via hierarchical objectives. Directly improves retrieval embeddings at low cost. **Exact analog**: Use deeper "privileged" computation (full diagnostics) to supervise shallower/normal adapter path.
- **AsymDSD / ATAS**: Asymmetric dual or any-to-any self-distill (masked + invariance, global-local). Momentum teacher. Strong for representation learning without external labels.
- **Contrastive hybrids**: InfoNCE + self-distill common (repo already has `SedimentationInfoNCELoss` + `HardNegativeMiner` from chelation_log collisions — gold for privileged negatives).

**Repo Baseline Losses (audited)**:
- `sedimentation_loss.py`: MSE, InfoNCE (batch sim matrix CE), Hybrid (MSE+InfoNCE weights).
- `online_updater.py`: Pluggable `TripletMarginOnlineLoss`, `InfoNCEOnlineLoss`, `CosineSimilarityOnlineLoss`, `AdaptiveMargin`, schedulers. Already supports online per-query adapter micro-updates from retrieval results (perfect on-policy substrate).
- Adapters expose `regularization_loss()` (Procrustes skew-Frobenius).
- No true **asymmetric privileged self-distill**, no **KL retention to base**, no **filtering on directive fitness**, no **clipped divergence**, no **EMA teacher** — these are the gaps OPSD/SDPO fill.

**Pain Points Directly Addressed** (from 02_chelation_system_audit.md + panel):
- Instability / KL shocks / divergence in multi-cycle sedimentation → clipping + KL reg + EMA teacher.
- Sample inefficiency + vanishing signals (all-good/all-bad batches) → dense privileged targets from directives even on "incorrect" (low-fitness) probes.
- Forgetting (Procrustes drift, NDCG regression) → explicit retention terms + bounded deltas + fixed/EMA teacher.
- Self-healing advisory only, never reinforced → directives become "f" for privileged teacher targets; accepted directives trigger on-policy distill steps.
- Quantization fragility → quant-aware terms + bounded corrections already in `BoundedAdapter`.

## 2. 8 Candidate Loss Formulations

### Candidate 1: Asymmetric Privileged-Context Residual Distillation (OPSD-Basic + SDPO Self-Teacher)
**Inspiration**: OPSD asymmetric conditioning + SDPO feedback-conditioned self-teacher. Student normal adapter; teacher = privileged target generator from directive + diagnostics + homeostatic push.

**Formulation** (for batch of embeddings):
```
student_emb = normalize( x + adapter_student(x) )
teacher_target = compute_privileged_target(x, directive, diagnostics, chelation_log)  # e.g. homeostatic + weighted synthetic embeds + fitness signal
delta = student_emb - x_norm
loss = MSE(student_emb, stopgrad(teacher_target)) 
     + λ_ret * MSE(student_emb, stopgrad(base_emb))   # retention
     + λ_reg * adapter.regularization_loss()
     + optional: β * ||delta||^2 clipped
```

**Pseudocode**:
```python
def asymmetric_privileged_residual_loss(adapter, x, base_emb, directive, diagnostics, chelation_log, 
                                        lambda_ret=0.3, lambda_reg=0.01, clip_delta=0.5):
    student_emb = adapter(x)  # or bounded_adapter
    teacher_target = compute_privileged_embedding_target(
        x, directive=directive, diagnostics=diagnostics, 
        chelation_log=chelation_log, fitness=diagnostics.get('fitness')
    )  # non-diff or stopgrad
    teacher_target = F.normalize(teacher_target, dim=-1)
    base_norm = F.normalize(base_emb, dim=-1)
    student_norm = F.normalize(student_emb, dim=-1)
    
    corr_loss = F.mse_loss(student_norm, teacher_target.detach())
    ret_loss = F.mse_loss(student_norm, base_norm.detach())
    reg = adapter.regularization_loss() if hasattr(adapter, 'regularization_loss') else 0.0
    
    delta = student_norm - base_norm
    delta_norm = torch.norm(delta, dim=1, keepdim=True)
    clipped_delta_loss = torch.mean( torch.clamp(delta_norm, max=clip_delta)**2 )
    
    total = corr_loss + lambda_ret * ret_loss + lambda_reg * reg + 0.1 * clipped_delta_loss
    return total, {'corr': corr_loss.item(), 'ret': ret_loss.item()}
```

**Theoretical Properties**: MSE on hypersphere is approx cosine distance (stable for normalized embs). Retention term is explicit "stay close to base" (like OPSD fixed teacher). Gradient variance lower than pure contrastive when targets are high-quality privileged. Forward-mode (matching target) is mode-covering in embedding space.

**Dynamics**: Fast early gains (like OPSD 100 steps) because directive provides dense "why this correction" signal even on mediocre probes. Retention prevents the "catastrophic forgetting" seen in current Procrustes runs. Risk: if privileged_target generator is noisy, teacher drift — mitigate with EMA on targets or directive quality gate.

**Applicability**: Works for all adapter types (MLP/LoRA/Procrustes). SelfEditDirective.synthetic_examples are embedded once to seed teacher_target. Integrates directly with `SelfEditSandboxExecutor` and `CandidateLedgerEntry` (use ledger metrics for weighting teacher_target).

**Pros**: Simple, dense signal, explicit retention, leverages existing homeostatic_target fn.  
**Cons**: Depends on quality of `compute_privileged...` (needs implementation); MSE can be less structure-preserving than contrastive.  
**Risk/Mitigation**: Poor directives → filter by `SelfHealingChelationConfig.reward_threshold` before loss.

### Candidate 2: JSD / KL on In-Batch Similarity Distributions (Full-Vocabulary OPSD Analog for Embeddings)
**Inspiration**: OPSD full-vocab divergence (every possible next-token) → here every possible in-batch "match" via softmax(cosine) matrix. MoSE hierarchical + contrastive self-distill.

**Formulation**:
```
sim_s[i,j] = softmax( (student_embs[i] @ student_embs[j].T) / τ )   # student view sim dist
sim_t[i,j] = softmax( (teacher_targets[i] @ teacher_targets[j].T) / τ )
loss = JSD_β(sim_t || sim_s)   or  α*KL(t||s) + (1-α)*KL(s||t)
     + retention + reg
```
Average over batch + over multiple "views" (query vs doc).

**Pseudocode** (extend existing `SedimentationInfoNCELoss`):
```python
class JSDSimilarityDistillLoss(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, temperature=0.07, beta=0.5, lambda_ret=0.2):
        ...
    def forward(self, student_embs, teacher_targets, base_embs):
        sim_s = torch.mm(student_embs, student_embs.t()) / self.tau
        sim_t = torch.mm(teacher_targets, teacher_targets.t()) / self.tau
        log_p_s = F.log_softmax(sim_s, dim=1)
        log_p_t = F.log_softmax(sim_t, dim=1)
        # JSD
        m = self.beta * sim_t + (1-self.beta) * sim_s
        log_m = F.log_softmax(m / self.tau, dim=1)  # approx
        jsd = 0.5 * (F.kl_div(log_m, log_p_t, log_target=True, reduction='batchmean') + 
                     F.kl_div(log_m, log_p_s, log_target=True, reduction='batchmean'))
        ret = F.mse_loss(student_embs, base_embs.detach())
        return jsd + self.lambda_ret * ret + adapter_reg
```

**Theoretical**: JSD is symmetric, bounded, less mode-seeking than reverse KL. On similarity matrix it preserves retrieval structure (exactly what InfoNCE does, but now teacher-guided on-policy). Information-geometric: matches "beliefs" over matches.

**Dynamics**: Excellent for retrieval (preserves ranking structure on student's own "on-policy" batch). Clipping analog: clip extreme sim values before softmax. Converges fast on structure; sample-efficient because every pair in batch provides signal (dense like full-vocab).

**Applicability**: Perfect drop-in for `HardNegativeMiner` + chelation_log collisions (privileged negatives in teacher view). SelfEditDirective can specify "focus dimensions" or synthetic pairs for sim matrix.

**Pros**: Builds directly on repo's strongest loss (InfoNCE); dense pairwise signal; structure-preserving.  
**Cons**: O(batch^2) compute (mitigate with sampled negatives or top-k); sensitive to τ.  
**Risk/Mitigation**: Use `distillation_topk` style: only top-k most similar in batch for JSD terms.

### Candidate 3: SDPO-Style Dense Advantage Distillation on Proxy Log-Probs
**Inspiration**: SDPO dense reward r_n = log pT - log pS ; policy gradient form. Here proxy "logprob" = -dist(emb, target) or log(sim).

**Formulation**:
```
for each sample i in on-policy batch:
    r_i = log_p_teacher_proxy(student_emb_i) - log_p_student_proxy(...)   # e.g. -||s - t|| or cos(s, privileged)
    loss += - r_i * log_p_student_proxy   (REINFORCE-style, or just MSE weighted by advantage)
```
Or directly: KL(student_proxy || teacher) but with advantage weighting.

**Pseudocode**:
```python
def sdpo_dense_advantage_loss(student_embs, teacher_targets, base_embs, adapter):
    # proxy logp ~ negative distance or cosine
    logp_s = -torch.norm(student_embs - base_embs, dim=1)   # or F.cosine_similarity
    logp_t = -torch.norm(teacher_targets - base_embs, dim=1)
    advantages = (logp_t - logp_s).detach()   # stopgrad teacher
    # weighted correction
    per_sample_loss = F.mse_loss(student_embs, teacher_targets.detach(), reduction='none').mean(dim=1)
    weighted = (advantages * per_sample_loss).mean()   # or policy gradient on adapter params
    return weighted + retention + adapter.regularization_loss()
```

**Theoretical**: Turns the distillation into advantage-weighted regression — higher credit to tokens/dims where teacher strongly prefers the direction. Reduces variance vs uniform MSE. Derivable as policy gradient on continuous "action" = delta.

**Dynamics**: Handles "incorrect" on-policy samples gracefully (negative advantage downweights bad corrections). Sample efficient; works with single "rollout" (one batch) like MIS-PO.

**Applicability**: Use `SelfEditEvaluation.reward` or `fitness.fitness - baseline` directly as scalar advantage multiplier. Directive metadata can provide per-example advantages.

**Pros**: Dense credit assignment (core SDPO win); naturally handles mixed good/bad batches; easy scalar feedback path.  
**Cons**: Proxy "logp" choice critical (needs calibration); can have high variance without clipping/filtering.  
**Risk/Mitigation**: Clip advantages + combine with Candidate 4 filtering.

### Candidate 4: Filtered + Pointwise-Clipped Self-Distillation (MIS-PO + OPSD Clip)
**Inspiration**: MIS-PO binary ratio filtering + OPSD per-entry pointwise clip + BoundedAdapter.

**Formulation**:
```
ratio = sigmoid( (fitness_gain - baseline) / delta_norm ) or similar "acceptance prob"
mask = (r_min < ratio < r_max) & quant_survival_ok
for unmasked: compute divergence contrib per "position" (dim or pair)
clipped_contrib = min( contrib, τ )
loss = mean( clipped_contrib * mask ) + small_kl_penalty( current_adapter || ref_adapter )
```

**Pseudocode** (inspired by ar0cket1 MIS-PO + OPSD clip):
```python
def filtered_clipped_distill_loss(student_embs, teacher_targets, fitness_gains, delta_norms, quant_survivals, 
                                  r_min=0.7, r_max=1.4, tau=2.0, kl_beta=0.05, ref_adapter_state=None):
    ratios = torch.sigmoid( (fitness_gains - 0.0) / (delta_norms + 1e-6) )
    mask = (ratios > r_min) & (ratios < r_max) & quant_survivals.bool()
    
    raw_div = (student_embs - teacher_targets.detach()) ** 2   # per-dim or per-sample
    clipped_div = torch.minimum(raw_div, torch.tensor(tau))
    filtered_loss = (clipped_div * mask.unsqueeze(-1)).mean()
    
    # MIS-PO style small KL to ref (or base)
    if ref_adapter_state is not None:
        kl_pen = 0.0  # compute param KL or output KL
    else:
        kl_pen = 0.0
    return filtered_loss + kl_beta * kl_pen
```

**Theoretical**: Filtering removes high-variance outliers (exactly MIS-PO motivation for stable single-sample online updates). Pointwise clip bounds the influence of any single "bad" dimension or stylistic collision — reduces gradient shock.

**Dynamics**: Extremely stable across sedimentation cycles (addresses documented multi-cycle NDCG regression). Lower effective batch size but much higher quality gradients. Enables safe continual adapter hot-swapping (like Hermes LoRA).

**Applicability**: Uses `SelfEditEvaluation.reward`, `quantization_gate`, `CandidateLedgerEntry.metrics`, `BoundedAdapter` min/max directly for mask. `structural_health_score` as additional filter signal.

**Pros**: Highest stability; directly solves variance/forgetting in current system; production-ready filtering.  
**Cons**: Can discard too many samples if thresholds strict (tune via config); requires rich metadata per sample (already provided by ledger).  
**Risk/Mitigation**: Adaptive r_min/r_max based on recent acceptance rate from ledger.

### Candidate 5: Multi-Objective Chelation (Correction + Retention + Structural + Quant)
**Inspiration**: Multi-task from OPSD ablations + repo's existing hybrid losses + quantization_promotion_gate + structural_health_score.

**Formulation** (weighted sum, with possible learned or scheduled weights):
```
L = w_corr * L_correction( student, teacher_priv ) 
  + w_ret * L_retention( student, base )
  + w_struct * (1 - structural_health( student_embs )) 
  + w_quant * L_quant_robust( quant_simulate(student), teacher )
  + w_reg * reg_loss
```

**Pseudocode**:
```python
def multi_objective_chelation_loss(..., weights={'corr':1.0, 'ret':0.4, 'struct':0.2, 'quant':0.15}):
    l_corr = asymmetric_privileged... (from #1)
    l_ret = F.mse_loss(...)
    l_struct = 1.0 - compute_structural_health(student_embs, diagnostics)  # reuse existing
    # quant robustness: add small noise simulating INT8, re-apply adapter or just to output
    student_quant = simulate_int8( student_embs )  # from evolution_strategies_optimizer
    l_quant = F.mse_loss( F.normalize(student_quant), teacher_targets.detach() )
    return sum(w * l for w,l in zip(weights.values(), [l_corr, l_ret, l_struct, l_quant])) + reg
```

**Theoretical**: Pareto front over competing goals (strong correction vs never-forget vs quant-survivable). Regularization via auxiliary tasks (common in stable distillation).

**Dynamics**: Balanced improvement; prevents over-optimization on one axis (e.g. pure correction destroying quant survival or structural health). Slower per-step but higher final quality and robustness.

**Applicability**: Leverages almost every existing diagnostic in the repo (`structural_health_score.py`, `quantization_promotion_gate.py`, `BoundedAdapter`). SelfEditDirective can carry per-directive weight suggestions in optimization_params.

**Pros**: Most comprehensive; directly optimizes the exact metrics used in `SelfEditEvaluation` and promotion gates.  
**Cons**: Weight tuning required (use evolution_strategies_optimizer or kalman scheduler); more compute.  
**Risk/Mitigation**: Dynamic weighting (e.g. higher ret when retention_score low per ledger).

### Candidate 6: Hierarchical / Multi-View Self-Distillation (MoSE + AsymDSD Inspired)
**Inspiration**: MoSE multi-exit hierarchical self-distill for early embeddings; asymmetric views (normal vs privileged directive view); momentum teacher.

**Formulation**: Multiple "exits" or views — e.g. raw base, lightly adapted, full directive-privileged. Distill higher-privilege view → lower views + cross-view invariance.

**Pseudocode** sketch:
```python
# views: v0=base, v1=adapter_light(directive_minimal), v2=full_privileged
loss = distill(v2, v1) + distill(v2, v0) + contrastive_cross_views(v0,v1,v2) + momentum_update(teacher_v2)
```

**Applicability**: Use different strength of `SelfEditDirective` (minimal vs full metadata) to create views. Or multiple adapters (router in `adapter_router.py`).

**Pros**: Improves "early" (light) corrections; enables modular deployment (cheap adapter vs rich).  
**Cons**: Requires multiple forward passes or clever sharing.

### Candidate 7: EMA Self-Teacher + Trust-Region Chelation (SDPO Config Practical)
**Inspiration**: SDPO `teacher_regularization=ema` (update_rate 0.05) + trust-region mixing; fixed teacher for stability in OPSD.

**Formulation**: Maintain `ema_adapter` (or ema_targets). Loss = distill( current_student, ema_teacher ) + trust_region_penalty( ||current - ema|| < delta_trust ).

Update ema after step: ema = (1-rate)*ema + rate*student .

**Pseudocode**:
```python
# in trainer loop
with torch.no_grad():
    ema_target = ema_teacher(x)
loss = distill_loss(student, ema_target) + kl_beta * output_kl(student, ema_target)  # or param trust
...
# after opt step
update_ema(ema_teacher, student_adapter, rate=0.05)
```

**Dynamics**: Slow-moving teacher provides stable target (like OPSD initial fixed + SDPO EMA). Trust region prevents large policy jumps (MIS-PO spirit).

**Pros**: Extremely stable for long-running self-healing loops; simple to implement on top of any adapter.  
**Cons**: Slight lag in teacher (but that's the point for retention).

## 3. Theoretical Properties, Dynamics, and Cross-Candidate Analysis

**Common Theoretical Strengths**:
- **On-policy + dense**: Every sample (even low-fitness) contributes via privileged teacher (unlike sparse reward RL or hard-filter STaR).
- **Asymmetric + stopgrad**: Prevents teacher from regressing; student chases improving target.
- **KL / divergence control**: JSD, clipped pointwise, retention KL, trust-region — all bound "KL shocks".
- **Filtering + clipping**: Variance reduction (MIS-PO) + influence bounding (OPSD clip).
- **Retention by construction**: Explicit terms or fixed/EMA teacher guarantee base model preservation (addresses Procrustes NDCG regression documented in panels).

**Expected Dynamics Across Variants**:
- **Speed**: Candidates 1,2,3,8 converge fastest (dense signal); 4,7 most stable/slow-burn.
- **Sample Efficiency**: 4–8× better than pure sedimentation or ES (per OPSD/SDPO numbers) because directives + ledger turn "waste" low-fitness probes into useful training signal.
- **Stability**: 4 and 7 win (filtering + EMA/trust). Risk of collapse highest in pure correction (1 without retention).
- **Quant/Retention Tradeoff**: 5 explicitly optimizes the promotion gates; 1+4 with BoundedAdapter excel.
- **Forgetting**: All with retention/EMA/fixed-teacher mitigate; pure InfoNCE without ret term (current repo) is riskiest.
